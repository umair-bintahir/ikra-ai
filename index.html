      How it works

      Learning doesn't move in a straight line.

      Every IKRA inquiry starts with a big question and follows the learner's own questions. AI adapts the journey to how each child is thinking, drawing on years of inquiry-based classroom practice.

      The learner iswonderingAI adapts every step

      1. A puzzle, a story or a surprising fact sparks real wonder, and the child's own questions.

        Learner, Grade 4If every bee disappeared, would anyone even notice?

      2. Children investigate ideas and evidence, and try different ways of seeing the problem.

        Learner, Grade 4Let's watch which flowers the bees visit, and what they carry away.

      3. They test assumptions, compare perspectives and decide what the evidence supports.

        Learner, Grade 4No bees means the pollen doesn't move. So fewer fruits and seeds?

      4. They notice how their thinking changed, and reflection opens the next question.

        Learner, Grade 4I thought bees just made honey. They actually hold the whole garden together.

      Illustrative example from a Grade 4 inquiry, "Why do bees matter?"

      AI supports the thinking. It never replaces it.

      Most AI rushes to the answer. IKRA holds it back while the child still has thinking to do, and explains clearly once they've done it. Pick a verb to see the difference.

      For learners

      Six thinking moves children keep for life.

      Thinking becomes a habit when children can hear themselves doing it. Every IKRA inquiry practises six moves, in the child's own words.

      I wonder…

      Why do bees visit some flowers and not others?

      Curiosity
      What if…?

      Imagination
      How do I know?

      Does the pattern really keep growing, or am I just guessing?

      Evidence
      Here's my reason…

      The ship floats because its shape pushes away lots of water.

      Reasoning
      Another way to see it…

      Maybe the bird isn't sad. Maybe it's just tired from flying.

      Perspective
      I changed my mind because…
